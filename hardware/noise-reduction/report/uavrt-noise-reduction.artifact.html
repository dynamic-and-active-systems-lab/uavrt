Stage

Calibrating the two receivers

Neither receiver reports power in real units, so on 18 November a signal generator fed a known tone at 160.1725 MHz into each, through the drone's own processing. That turns every recording into dBm and measures each receiver's own noise. It also showed how each copes with a strong signal: step the tone up and watch the Mini's noise rise around it while the HF+ stays flat.

How much of what's left is the drone?

Noise powers add. In flight the antenna delivers outside noise (from the drone and from the surroundings), the coax and choke attenuate it, and the receiver adds its own. Move the sliders to see how much outside noise it takes to raise the floor by a given amount. The present flight rise of 3.3 dB needs only about 10 dB of outside noise above thermal, which is ordinary background for a semi-rural site.

Methods

Noise floor from a recording

Every test used the drone's own receive chain. The detector writes raw complex samples at 3750 samples/s (Mini) or 3840 samples/s (HF+). Each second is cut into blocks of N = 256 samples, Hann-windowed:

Pm(fk) = |Σn w[n] xm[n] e−j2πkn/N|² / (fs Σn w[n]²)
N̂ = median over the inner 80 % of bins of [ median over blocks of Pm(fk) ]

The median over blocks removes tag pulses (20 ms in every 1.33 s). It reads about 1.6 dB under the mean, a bias that cancels because the calibration uses the same estimator. Script: tools/noise_floor.py.

Calibration and noise figure

Tone power is the spectrum above the local noise, summed over the tone's bins; the gain from dBm at the input to receiver units follows from the generator setting and the 0.6 dB path loss:

S = Σk∈tone (P̄(fk) − Nlocal) Δf     G = 10 log₁₀ S − (Pgen − Lpath)
Nin = 10 log₁₀ Nload − G     NF = Nin − (−173.9 dBm/Hz at 295 K)
Nflight = Nin,load + (mflight − mload)

The last line converts a flight's floor m, in receiver units, to dBm/Hz through the same receiver's calibration. Script: tools/bench_calibration.py.

Comparing Mini and HF+ readings directly

Each receiver reports power in its own arbitrary units. The calibration gives each one's gain G from dBm at its input to its units, and the difference between the two gains converts one receiver's units into the other's:

ΔG = GMini − GHF+ = 59.86 − 24.11 = 35.7 dB     mHF+ units = mMini units − ΔG

It holds because both readings are per hertz, so the different sample rates (3750 and 3840 samples/s) drop out; both use the same estimator, whose bias differed by only 0.04 dB between the receivers; and the same generator, cable and choke fed both, so their errors cancel in the difference. It applies at the calibrated settings only: Mini gain 21 and the HF+'s fixed settings. Ponui used Mini gain 20.

Worked example. Ponui flight 3 in steady flight read −86.9 in Mini units, which is −86.9 − 35.7 = −122.6 in HF+ units. Flight F2 on 14 November read −141.1 in HF+ units. The 18.5 dB difference agrees within rounding with the 18.4 dB from converting both to dBm/Hz.

Outside noise and the noise budget

With noise factor F = 10NF/10 and outside noise Fa, both relative to kT₀, and a feed of loss L at room temperature:

Fa′ = Fa/L + (1 − 1/L)     rise = 10 log₁₀[(Fa′ + F − 1) / F]     Fa′ = F·10rise/10 − (F − 1)
ITU-R P.372: Fam = c − d log₁₀ fMHz   (city 76.8/27.7, residential 72.5/27.7, rural 67.2/27.7, quiet rural 53.6/28.6, galactic 52.0/23.0)

Free-space detection range scales as 10−rise/20.

Cable measurements

Insertion loss = −20 log₁₀|S₂₁|; return loss = −20 log₁₀|S₁₁|; mismatch loss = −10 log₁₀(1 − |S₁₁|²). Each series pair read more than the sum of its parts by the calibration thru cable's loss (0.05–0.17 dB, with 1.5 ns of extra delay), which identifies the correction.

Caveats and next tests

Known limits

  • Different sites: rural New Zealand and semi-rural Arizona have different background noise.
  • Ponui ran the Mini at gain 20; the calibration used 21. Likely 1–2 dB on the Ponui values.
  • No telemetry for the 2026 flights; heights also differed between configurations.
  • On the ground the antenna lies on the soil and hears little outside noise, so "ground" is not quite "everything except the motors".
  • Absolute levels are good to a few tenths of a dB; the comparison between receivers is tighter.

Planned

  • Same-day flights alternating HF+, Mini, HF+ with choke and 15 ft coax: does the Mini pick up out-of-band signals the HF+ rejects?
  • Background reference: antenna on a mast, drone off; then drone powered at the base, motors off.
  • A strong-signal pass toward tag-like pulses from a Tabor LS3081P generator.
  • PX4 flight logs, to replace inferred flight stages with altitude and throttle.
