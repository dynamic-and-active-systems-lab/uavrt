How much quieter the drone got

Michael W. Shafer, Northern Arizona University. Analysis and drafting assisted by Claude. 2026 tests at 160.172 MHz; field data from 2025 at 160–173 MHz.

The ferrites on the USB cable

Ponui Day 2, 17 March 2025: four flights with the Mini hanging outside the shield on a USB extension with no ferrites. On the 18th it rained, cabin tests showed ferrites on the USB cable helped, and every later flight carried two snap-on ferrites on it. Same Mini at gain 20, same island, same aircraft. In flight the noise fell 10.4 dB; with the motors off it fell about 17 dB.

  • Without the ferrites the drone was loud even standing still: −143 to −149 dBm/Hz on the ground with the motors off, as loud as the later flights were in the air. That noise came from the Pi and avionics along the USB cable, through the Mini and down the outside of the coax.
  • Cumbria, November 2025 (Mini inside the shield, bonded antenna bulkhead, choke): −158 dBm/Hz in flight. The noise rose as the motors spun up on the ground, stayed level from a few metres to 95 m, and peaked through the final descent and landing. The PX4 logs show it then outlasted the motors by about 15–17 s before dropping to the receiver's own level: something other than the motors was running after disarm. What the flight logs add

The PX4 logs give each 2026 flight's height and motor state, aligned to the noise to the second.

  • With the choke and 15 ft coax, the motors at hover power add nothing measurable while the antenna lies on the ground. F3 ended with about 15 s of hovering 0.9 m up, motors at hover thrust, antenna on the ground: −166.5 to −166.9 dBm/Hz, the receiver's own noise. At 45–53 m the same flights read −163.5. The 3.3 dB rise appears only with the antenna in the air.
  • The noise peaks when the drone is 5–12 m up, with the antenna dangling near the ground: up to −158 dBm/Hz in F2 and F3. These are the bursts at takeoff and landing.
  • Without the choke or the extension, the drone is noisier near the ground than at height: F5 −156.5 in the final descent against −161.8 at height, F6 −153.6 against −156.6, F7 −158.7 against −161.5.
  • The noise does not follow the motor current: correlation between −0.26 and +0.23 in steady flight on every flight.
  • Heights: F2 53 m, F3 45 m, F5 28 m, F6 and F7 18–19 m above the takeoff point. The notes' 50 ft for F5–F7 was the planned height.

What the noise looks like across the channel

The detector listens to a 3.84 kHz-wide channel. At Ponui the flight noise was flat across it. Now the floor is low but a few narrow interference tones remain, strongest without the choke.

Neither receiver reports power in real units, so on 18 November a signal generator fed a known tone at 160.1725 MHz into each, through the drone's own processing. That turns every recording into dBm and measures each receiver's own noise. Methods

Noise floor from a recording

Every test used the drone's own receive chain. The detector writes raw complex samples at 3750 samples/s (Mini) or 3840 samples/s (HF+). Each second is cut into blocks of N = 256 samples, Hann-windowed:

Pm(fk) = |Σn w[n] xm[n] e−j2πkn/N|² / (fs Σn w[n]²)
N̂ = median over the inner 80 % of bins of [ median over blocks of Pm(fk) ]

The median over blocks removes tag pulses (20 ms in every 1.33 s). It reads about 1.6 dB under the mean, a bias that cancels because the calibration uses the same estimator. Script: tools/noise_floor.py.

Noise from the detector's pulse logs

Most 2025 flights have no IQ recording, but every pulse the detector reports carries its own noise estimate for the channel, noise_psd, in the recording's units, logged by TagTracker with the aircraft's position. It is a mean, so it reads 1.59 dB above the median-based estimator. For the Mini:

N = 10 log₁₀(noise_psd) − 59.81 dBm/Hz     −59.81 = −167.58 − (−109.36 + 1.59)

Checked against the IQ recordings of Ponui flights P2 and P3: −145.7 and −145.5 from the pulse logs against −145.5 and −145.1, within 0.4 dB. Only flights and moments with detections count. Script: tools/pulse_log_noise.py.

Calibration and noise figure

Tone power is the spectrum above the local noise, summed over the tone's bins; the gain from dBm at the input to receiver units follows from the generator setting and the 0.6 dB path loss:

S = Σk∈tone (P̄(fk) − Nlocal) Δf     G = 10 log₁₀ S − (Pgen − Lpath)
Nin = 10 log₁₀ Nload − G     NF = Nin − (−173.9 dBm/Hz at 295 K)
Nflight = Nin,load + (mflight − mload)

The last line converts a flight's floor m, in receiver units, to dBm/Hz through the same receiver's calibration. Script: tools/bench_calibration.py.

Comparing Mini and HF+ readings directly

Each receiver reports power in its own arbitrary units. The calibration gives each one's gain G from dBm at its input to its units, and the difference between the two gains converts one receiver's units into the other's:

ΔG = GMini − GHF+ = 59.86 − 24.11 = 35.7 dB     mHF+ units = mMini units − ΔG

It holds because both readings are per hertz, so the different sample rates (3750 and 3840 samples/s) drop out; both use the same estimator, whose bias differed by only 0.04 dB between the receivers; and the same generator, cable and choke fed both, so their errors cancel in the difference. It applies at the calibrated settings only: Mini gain 21 and the HF+'s fixed settings. Ponui used Mini gain 20.

Worked example. Ponui flight 3 in steady flight read −86.9 in Mini units, which is −86.9 − 35.7 = −122.6 in HF+ units. Flight F2 on 14 November read −141.1 in HF+ units. The 18.5 dB difference agrees within rounding with the 18.4 dB from converting both to dBm/Hz.

Outside noise and the noise budget

With noise factor F = 10NF/10 and outside noise Fa, both relative to kT₀, and a feed of loss L at room temperature:

Fa′ = Fa/L + (1 − 1/L)     rise = 10 log₁₀[(Fa′ + F − 1) / F]     Fa′ = F·10rise/10 − (F − 1)
ITU-R P.372: Fam = c − d log₁₀ fMHz   (city 76.8/27.7, residential 72.5/27.7, rural 67.2/27.7, quiet rural 53.6/28.6, galactic 52.0/23.0)

Free-space detection range scales as 10−rise/20.

Cable measurements

Insertion loss = −20 log₁₀|S₂₁|; return loss = −20 log₁₀|S₁₁|; mismatch loss = −10 log₁₀(1 − |S₁₁|²). Each series pair read more than the sum of its parts by the calibration thru cable's loss (0.05–0.17 dB, with 1.5 ns of extra delay), which identifies the correction.

Caveats and next tests

Known limits

  • Different sites and frequencies: Ponui (160–161 MHz), Cumbria (173 MHz) and Arizona (160 MHz) differ in background noise. Stages 3 and 4 include that; the USB-ferrite step does not.
  • Cumbria ran at 173 MHz and Mini gain 21; the calibration was at 160 MHz.
  • Ponui ran the Mini at gain 20; the calibration used 21. Likely 1–2 dB on the Ponui values.
  • Heights differed between configurations: 45–53 m with the choke and 15 ft coax, 28 m without the choke, 18–19 m without the extension.
  • On the ground the antenna lies on the soil and hears little outside noise, so "ground" is not quite "everything except the motors".
  • Absolute levels are good to a few tenths of a dB; the comparison between receivers is tighter.

Planned

  • Same-day flights alternating HF+, Mini, HF+ with choke and 15 ft coax: does the Mini pick up out-of-band signals the HF+ rejects?
  • Background reference: antenna on a mast, drone off; then drone powered at the base, motors off.
  • A strong-signal pass toward tag-like pulses from a Tabor LS3081P generator.
  • Keep recording about 30 s after landing (detection now stops at disarm), to see whether anything still runs after disarm, as it did in Cumbria.
